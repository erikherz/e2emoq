Run a partner fleet

Spin up your own relay and join e2eMoQ's geo-brokered CDN. Your box becomes a regional edge that serves nearby viewers — carrying media it can never read. Onboarding is by request.

By request. Partner fleets are added on request. Contact erik@vivoh.com to be issued installer access and a customer delegation, then follow the steps below.

What you're joining

e2eMoQ's CDN is brokered: a lightweight broker (cdnadmin, at moqcdn.net/cdnadmin) holds the fleet registry and picks a box per viewer. Once your fleet is delegated, geo-located, and resynced, the broker ranks it by great-circle distance from each viewer and routes nearby traffic to it. Your box then either serves as the origin for broadcasters near you, or cluster-pulls a remote origin over host:port to serve viewers near you — automatically, no per-stream configuration.

All media stays relay-blind, end-to-end encrypted: as an edge you move ciphertext you cannot decrypt, and access is enforced by a per-broadcast token the relay verifies with a public key only (BYOK) — your box can verify tokens but never mint them. Running a partner fleet grants you no ability to watch, forge, or tamper with the streams flowing through it.

The encryption is what confines a stream to its own app: the content key lives only in the origin app's Worker and reaches only that app's authorized viewers, so the only people who can access a stream are those watching from the same app that created it. Your edge simply moves ciphertext to those viewers — it never holds the key, and neither does anyone on any other app or domain.

Your box is also scoped to only the customers you agree to host. The broker pushes a tenant record (its name and public verify key) to your box only for customers on your allow-list, and each provisioning bearer is host-scoped — bound to your hostname, so it is worthless on any other box. A partner box therefore sees nothing about other customers or other fleets: no shared registry, no fleet-wide credential, no customer list.

Before you start

  • A server (VPS or bare metal) with a public IPv4 and root/sudo.
  • A domain you control, so you can point a hostname at the box and it can obtain TLS.
  • The ability to open the relay's UDP QUIC ports (the 800X range) in any host or cloud firewall.
  • An installer invite + a customer delegation from the e2eMoQ operator (see the callout above).

Steps

  1. Spin up a server and map DNS

    Point relay.yourdomain.com at the box's public IP. Choose a hostname that names the box's region or city — the broker can derive coordinates from it (e.g. ams, sjc), or you set them explicitly in step 6.

  2. Run the installer

    Installs the hermit-moq relay + the moqcdn autoscaler, opens the QUIC ports, and provisions TLS:

    curl -fsSL https://moqcdn.net/partners/install-brokered.sh | bash -s -- you@domain.com relay.domain.com moqcdn.net

    Pass your email, your relay hostname, and the broker domain (moqcdn.net) the box will delegate to. When it finishes it prints an admin login — the email you gave plus a generated password. Save them; they are shown once.

  3. Open your box's admin console

    Browse to https://relay.yourdomain.com and sign in with the email you passed to the installer and the password it printed.

    These are your credentials for your box — keep them private. Never share or publish them; anyone who has them can control your fleet's delegation.

  4. Delegate the fleet to the broker

    In the box admin, delegate the fleet to moqcdn.net and copy the delegation token it generates.

  5. Register the fleet in cdnadmin

    Go to moqcdn.net/cdnadmin → Add fleet (/fleet) and paste the delegation token from the previous step.

  6. Set the coordinates for geo-steering

    Add the box's latitude / longitude so the broker can geo-rank it. Without coordinates the fleet falls into flat, distance-less ordering and is never picked as “nearest” for any viewer — it would only ever receive spill-over.

  7. Re-push all customers

    cdnadmin → Customers (/customers) → “Re-push all customers.” This writes each customer's provisioning credential onto your newly delegated box so it can actually serve their streams.

    Don't skip this — until it runs, your box rejects assignment requests with 401 and the broker silently spills traffic to other fleets, so your fleet appears delegated but never serves anything.

Verify it's serving

Force a viewer to your fleet with the ?geo= override (its own coordinates) against any live broadcast and confirm it plays:

https://e2emoq.com/<stream-id>?geo=<your-lat>,<your-lon>

The broker should route the viewer to relay.yourdomain.com:80xx and the stream should reach first frame — served directly if the broadcaster is on your box, or cluster-pulled from a remote origin if not.
